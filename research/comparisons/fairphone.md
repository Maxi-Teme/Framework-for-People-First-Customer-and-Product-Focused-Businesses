## 1. Summary

Fairphone **confirms** the framework's transparency principle more fully than any company here except Buffer:
- full audited accounts, published by a private company, including the going-concern warning;
- supplier and smelter lists with failed audits;
- an impact report that lists missed targets;
- per-phone cost breakdowns for its first two phones, one showing €9 net profit per phone.

It **adds** the supply-chain practices FD-08 needed:
- the verification level is stated next to each claim (certified, second-party, or documents only);
- a living-wage bonus is paid to all low-paid workers at supplier factories;
- the claim is framed honestly: "fairer", not "fair".

It shows **limits** the framework should plan for:
- per-product cost disclosure was dropped as the company grew;
- "fair" percentages depend on credits and mass balance;
- transparency and fair sourcing cost money a small company may not have (losses, 12 % secured loans, a going-concern emphasis).

## 3. Practices in detail

### FAI-01: Cost breakdowns

- **What the company does:**
  - **Fairphone 1 (2013):** €325 split into €185 product, €45 operations and €22 "interventions".
  - **Fairphone 2 (2015):** €525 average price; Fairphone kept €407 after VAT, fees and resellers, and earned €9 net per phone. Called "an exercise in transparency and not an official accounting exercise".
  - **Since then:** no per-phone breakdown has been published.

  [18, 20]
- **Fit:** partial. It is the framework's cost disclosure at its best, then dropped.
- **Lesson for FD-04:** make the breakdown a standing practice with each product, not a launch-time exercise. Everlane's breakdown (EVL-01) faded the same way.

### FAI-02: Full audited accounts

- **What the company does:** the full annual report is published on its website, including director pay, the auditor's emphasis of matter on going concern, and 12 % secured shareholder loans. Preliminary figures that differed from the audited ones remain public [1, 3].
- **Fit:** match. It is the minimum public set FD-03 asks for, published by a private company.

### FAI-03: Supplier and smelter lists

- **What the company does:** it publishes Tier-1, Tier-2 and some Tier-3 suppliers, plus 144 smelters and refiners with their audit status: 93 passed, 6 failed, and a Russian refiner marked "unable to proceed" [2].
- **Fit:** match. Goes further than Nike's factory list (NKE-02) by including outcomes.

### FAI-04: Fair materials, method disclosed

- **What the company does:** 51 % fair or recycled materials by weight (Gen 6). Cobalt counts 100 % "via credits"; Fairtrade gold is "not necessarily used within the specific component". Each figure has a colour-coded verification level [2].
- **Fit:** partial. An honest method, but much of the share isn't physical content.
- **Transfer:** for FD-08, state the method and verification level next to every impact number.

### FAI-05: Living-wage bonus at suppliers

- **What the company does:** since 2019 it pays the gap to a living wage for hours worked on its line, shared among all low-paid workers in the factory. 2025: $215,000 to 1,650 workers. Living wage costs "just over $1" per phone [2].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-02 and FD-08: a concrete, costed commitment to supplier workers. Compare Patagonia's living-wage share (PAT-10) and Nike's disputed wages (NKE-05).

### FAI-06: E-waste neutrality

- **What the company does:** for each product sold, it collects an equal weight of e-waste: 30 tonnes in 2025. Only 2 % came from its own take-back; legally required collection is counted [2].
- **Fit:** partial. A measurable target, with a contestable method.

### FAI-07: Impact report with misses

- **What the company does:** it publishes targets and results, including misses: emissions −7.3 % against −12 %, average lifespan 4.1 years against 4.5. Some indicators are third-party assured [2].
- **Fit:** match. Like Patagonia (PAT-06).

### FAI-08: Long support

- **What the company does:** support grew from about 3.5 years (Fairphone 1, ended early in 2017) to 7 years (Fairphone 2 and 3) and 8 years (Gen 6, to 2033) [21, 22, 24, 25].
- **Fit:** partial. FD-07: a long-term commitment, learned from breaking an early one.

### FAI-09: Repairability

- **What the company does:** modular design; iFixit 10/10 for Gen 6 [1].
- **Fit:** gap. Like Framework Computer (FRM-07).

### FAI-10: Open software

- **What the company does:** bootloader unlocking and published source code, though Gen 6 can't be fully built from source. Alternative operating systems are supported, and one is sold with warranty through Murena [27, 35].
- **Fit:** partial. Open where the supply chain allows.

### FAI-11: Community

- **What the company does:** a forum, volunteer "Angels", and crowdfunded production and equity. Early decisions were prioritised by "extensively engaging our community members" (2013). No formal input mechanism was found since [16, 18, 32, 34].
- **Fit:** partial. Community as funders and helpers more than as product decision-makers.

### FAI-12: Funding and fragility

- **What the company does:**
  - €49M from impact investors (2023).
  - Crowdfunding equity (2018) and an employee investment plan.
  - 2025: 12 % shareholder loans secured on receivables, inventory and IP; negative equity; going-concern emphasis.
  - A Series C of at least €50M open since September 2026.

  [1, 7, 9, 32]
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01: impact capital, but on hard terms.

### FAI-13: Honest framing

- **What the company does:** van Abel: "It would kill the brand if we'd claim the Fairphone were 100 percent fair." The company uses "fairer" [27, 33].
- **Fit:** match. The opposite of Everlane's "radical transparency" claims (EVL-02).

### FAI-14: Own staff

- **What the company does:** all staff are paid a living wage. People metrics are published: turnover 9 %, satisfaction 73 %, gender split 49/51 [1, 2].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-02.

## 4. Where the framework goes further than this company

- Ongoing per-product cost disclosure (stopped after 2015).
- A public roadmap and formal community input into product decisions.

## 5. Candidates for explicit rejection

None. Two practices to adapt:
- **FAI-04:** fairness claims via credits; keep the method and verification level next to the claim.
- **FAI-06:** counting legally required collection towards a voluntary target; count only what goes beyond the law.

## 6. Open questions for the decisions

- **FD-08:** should the framework require the verification level next to every impact claim (Fairphone's colour code)?
- **FD-04:** how can a small company keep per-product cost disclosure going as product lines multiply?
- **FD-01:** how can mission-driven hardware companies raise capital without 12 % secured debt?
