## 1. Summary

Buffer **confirms** the framework's transparency principles in practice, for more than a decade and through a cash crisis, a founder split and a three-year decline: open metrics, open shareholder updates, a public cost breakdown of its price and a public roadmap. It **adds** what the framework doesn't cover yet: pay and profit-sharing rules for employees, an ownership strategy (buying out investors whose terms pointed elsewhere), a deliberate choice of calm, profitable growth, and fairness rules beyond price (cancelling unused subscriptions, counting lost customers honestly). There is **no practice where it differs** from the framework.

Two findings matter most for the decisions. First, cost disclosure didn't stop Buffer from raising prices and cutting features in 2020–2023, which cost it customers: disclosure needs a rule behind it (FD-04). Second, Buffer's transparency is protected mainly by one founder's majority stake: a person, not a structure (FD-01). Buffer also stops short of the framework in two places: its task boards and its governance (board, decisions) aren't public.

## 3. Practices in detail

### BUF-01: "Default to transparency" as the first value

- **What Buffer does:** Transparency is listed first among six values and called the "true North Star" [1].
- **Why it works for them:** It's backed by concrete publications (BUF-02, 04, 05, 06), not just stated.
- **Fit:** match with framework principle 1.
- **Transfer:** Confirms the principle; the lesson is to name the publications that make it real (FD-06).

### BUF-02: Open salaries

- **What Buffer does:** Publishes its salary formula and every salary by name since December 2013, revised six times since [3].
- **Why it works for them:** Fully remote, formula-based pay, no negotiation; transparency was a condition of joining.
- **Fit:** partial. The framework's transparency covers it in spirit but doesn't mention pay; employees are glossary only.
- **Transfer:** Publish the formula and bands. Publishing names is a separate decision because of privacy and hiring effects.
- **Candidate to adapt:** formula and bands public; individual names optional or not at all.

### BUF-03: Pay formula

- **What Buffer does:** Role benchmark, 2 cost-of-living bands (90 % and 100 %), above-market pay, no negotiation; principles: transparent, simple, fair, generous [3].
- **Fit:** gap.
- **Transfer:** A pay principle for the employees topic (FD-02).

### BUF-04: Live public metrics

- **What Buffer does:** Revenue, customers and users are shown live on its website [1, 2].
- **Fit:** match.
- **Transfer:** A concrete mechanism and measure for principle 1 (FD-06). Note: the counters are undated and definitions can change (see BUF-15).

### BUF-05: Monthly public shareholder updates

- **What Buffer does:** Monthly updates with revenue, net income, cash and team size; burn rate reported until Buffer became profitable in January 2025 [16, 19]. No update listed for 2026 as of 12 October [20].
- **Why it works for them:** A small, private company with founder control; investors and public get the same information.
- **Fit:** match with transparency and accountability.
- **Transfer:** A strong mechanism. The 2026 gap shows the risk: a habit, not a rule, can stop quietly. The framework could say what is published and how often, and what happens if it stops.

### BUF-06: Transparent pricing

- **What Buffer does:** 2014: what each $10 pays for, line by line, including 46 cents of profit [5]. Today: a percentage breakdown of costs, with no profit line and no period stated [4].
- **Why it works for them:** Software has no obvious unit cost, so the breakdown makes the price feel less arbitrary [5].
- **Fit:** match with principle 3 (cost disclosure).
- **But:** the disclosure didn't stop the 2020–2023 move to higher prices and fewer features that cost customers [21]; it explains costs, not how the price is set. The current page also shows less than the 2014 one (no profit, no date).
- **Transfer:** Strong evidence for FD-04: keep the breakdown, always include profit and the period, and add a rule for how prices are set and changed.

### BUF-07: Profit sharing

- **What Buffer does:** 8–15 % of net profit to the team, plus an amount equal to 20 % of that share to charity, in profitable years [4]; for 2024, $416 per person on average [16].
- **Fit:** gap.
- **Transfer:** Answers part of FD-04's open question "where do gains go" and FD-02 (employees share in success).

### BUF-08: Public roadmap

- **What Buffer does:** Anyone with an account can suggest and upvote ideas; every request is public; statuses include "decided not to pursue". The team decides "if and when" [13, 14].
- **Why it works for them:** Votes inform but don't bind; the team keeps responsibility.
- **Fit:** match with principles 2 and 4.
- **Transfer:** Evidence for FD-05: open input plus an accountable team decision. Gap: Buffer doesn't publish *why* it picks or rejects a suggestion; the framework could ask for that.

### BUF-09: Public fundraising terms and open equity

- **What Buffer does:** Published its 2014 term sheet ($3.5M at $60M) [7] and its equity formula with each person's stake [6].
- **Fit:** partial. The framework asks for transparent governance but says nothing about ownership.
- **Transfer:** If a framework company raises money, the terms are public (FD-01, FD-03).

### BUF-10: Buying out investors

- **What Buffer does:** In 2018 it spent $3.3M, about half its cash, to buy back shares from 7 of 16 Series A investors whose terms (a 9 % annual return protection, a liquidity veto) pointed to a venture path; 21.2 % of fully diluted shares repurchased since 2017 [11, 15].
- **Why it works for them:** Profitable, with enough cash; willing investors.
- **Fit:** gap.
- **Transfer:** One concrete option for FD-01: choose investors whose terms fit, and keep a way to buy them out.

### BUF-11: Founder majority control and one direction

- **What Buffer does:** The founder holds about 57 % of issued shares [15]; after the 2017 split Buffer chose one path: long-term, sustainable, remote [10].
- **Why it works for them:** Fast, consistent decisions on values.
- **Fit:** gap.
- **Risk:** the protection depends on one person. Employees own 1.16 % of issued shares; succession is unknown.
- **Candidate to adapt:** founder control is fine early on, but the framework should add a structural lock-in that doesn't depend on one person.

### BUF-12: Calm, profitable growth

- **What Buffer does:** After 2016–2017: slower hiring, profitability over growth rate, turned down its largest acquisition offer (2014) [10, 11, 12].
- **Fit:** gap.
- **Transfer:** Confirms Toyota's long-term principle (TOY-12) from a small software company (FD-07).

### BUF-13: Public post-mortems

- **What Buffer does:** Explained the 2016 layoffs publicly with figures [8]; the founder talks openly about the 2020–2023 mistakes [21].
- **Criticism:** Bernoff called the 2016 post oversharing: too long, too much self-blame, too much "I" [9].
- **Fit:** partial.
- **Transfer:** Adopt public post-mortems, with Bernoff's point as a format rule: regular and short beats one long confession.

### BUF-14: Fully remote and a 4-day week

- **What Buffer does:** Fully remote since 2012–2013; 4-day week since 2020, kept through the recovery [1, 21].
- **Fit:** gap.
- **Transfer:** Choices for one company, not general principles; likely `park`.

### BUF-15: Fairness beyond price

- **What Buffer does:** In July 2025 it cancelled over 1,300 inactive legacy annual subscriptions; from August 2025 lost customers count the day they cancel, which lowered reported revenue [18].
- **Why it works for them:** Trust and accurate numbers matter more than the headline figure.
- **Fit:** gap. The framework's fair pricing is about cost disclosure, not about charging people who don't use the product.
- **Transfer:** FD-04 (don't charge people who get no value) and FD-06 (honest measures).

### BUF-16: "Down and wide"

- **What Buffer does:** After the decline: individual creators and small businesses, many social networks, the free plan back as the main entry [21].
- **Why it works for them:** Social media scheduling is a broad, price-sensitive market; chasing bigger customers lost the core.
- **Fit:** partial with the framework's customer focus.
- **Transfer:** FD-04: pricing for breadth (free plan, low entry price) is part of fairness. Meta lesson: adopted advice that "belonged to someone else's business" [21].

### BUF-17: Few management layers

- **What Buffer does:** Ended its self-management experiment in 2017 [10]; in 2022 Gascoigne stopped hiring executives and took the direct reports himself to stay close to the work [21].
- **Fit:** gap. The framework says nothing about structure or who decides.
- **Transfer:** FD-05: closeness to the work as a criterion for decision rights.

## 4. Where the framework goes further than Buffer

- **Open task boards:** Buffer publishes its roadmap, not its working boards (framework principle 4).
- **Open governance:** board decisions and the reasons for roadmap choices aren't public (principle 1).
- **Price justified by cost:** Buffer shows costs but doesn't derive the price from them (principle 3).
- **Community-driven development:** the community can suggest and vote, not co-develop (principle 2).

Evidence for or against these still has to come from other studies, for example GitLab (CS-003) on open boards and Framework Computer (CS-004) on prices.

## 5. Candidates for explicit rejection

Final decisions are in `../decisions.md`.

- BUF-02, the names part only: publishing every salary by name. Keep the formula and bands public; decide whether names are needed.
- BUF-11 as the *only* protection: founder majority control. Keep the goal, add a structure.
- BUF-14 (remote, 4-day week): probably park rather than reject; they're good choices, not principles.

## 6. Open questions for the decisions

- Does the framework need a rule against quietly stopping a publication (BUF-05)?
- Should the price breakdown always include profit and a date (BUF-06)?
- How much ownership should employees have, given Buffer's 1.16 % (BUF-11)?
- Should the reasons behind roadmap decisions be public, not only the statuses (BUF-08)?
