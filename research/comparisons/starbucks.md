## 1. Summary

Starbucks **adds** the clearest evidence for FD-07: twice, growth and efficiency eroded the product until customers left. The first time, in 2007, Schultz called it "the watering down of the Starbucks experience". The second, in 2026, Niccol said Starbucks was run "like a manufacturing facility". It also **adds** a cautionary case for FD-05. My Starbucks Idea collected about 160,000 customer ideas and implemented about 300 (2008–2018), then closed without a public successor. And it adds strong evidence for FD-02 from the other side: equity and tuition for part-time staff for over 30 years, but no first union contract in five years and a CEO pay ratio of 1,794:1.

It **confirms** almost nothing of the framework directly: no cost disclosure, no public roadmap, no open project management. Its turnarounds restored the customer experience through more labour and a simpler menu, but not through openness.

## 3. Practices in detail

### SBX-01: My Starbucks Idea

- **What the company does:** from 2008 customers posted and voted on ideas. By 2013 there were more than 150,000 ideas and 277 implemented, including cake pops, free Wi-Fi and mobile payment. An academic count found 162,156 ideas and 320 implemented, about 1 in 500. It was retired in 2018; no company statement on why was found [33, 34, 35, 36].
- **Fit:** partial. It is community input as the framework imagines it, with a visible output. It ended without a public successor, roadmap or explanation.
- **Lesson for FD-05:** a platform without a commitment to keep it, and without published reasons for decisions, can be switched off quietly. This is the same pattern as GitLab's mission change (GIT-03).

### SBX-02: Naming the failure

- **What the company does:**
  - **2007:** Schultz's memo said growth led to "the watering down of the Starbucks experience". He took "full responsibility"; the memo was internal and leaked.
  - **2026:** Niccol, in a podcast: "We got really focused on trying to be efficient and run it like a manufacturing facility, as opposed to recognizing, no, this is actually a customer service experience."

  [4, 5, 56]
- **Fit:** partial. It is honest diagnosis, like Toyota's hansei (TOY-15), but each time it came after the damage and wasn't a standing practice.

### SBX-03: Closing every store to retrain

- **What the company does:** on 26 February 2008, all 7,100 US stores closed for about three hours to retrain about 135,000 staff [8].
- **Fit:** gap. It is the retail version of Toyota's "stop the line" (TOY-02): stop everything to fix quality, visibly.

### SBX-04: Price freeze and upcharge removed

- **What the company does:**
  - **Prices:** no menu price increases at company stores in North America through FY2025, and the non-dairy upcharge removed [38, 60].
  - **Niccol:** pricing is "one of those things that we do as a last resort" [62].
  - **CFO for FY2027:** "Any pricing will remain focused and well below CPI" [22].
- **Fit:** partial. It is a public price commitment with a reference point (inflation), but without costs behind it.

### SBX-05: No cost disclosure

- **What the company does:** no item costs are published; North American ticket +5.0 % in Q4 FY2026 came from "product mix and customer food attach, pricing and higher delivery sales" [18].
- **Fit:** differs from principle 3.

### SBX-06: Back to the core

- **What the company does:**
  - Menu cut by about 30 %, using a "stage gate" for "fewer, better" items.
  - More than $500M of extra labour hours.
  - Seats, condiment bars and ceramic cups back.
  - Pickup-only stores closed.

  [44, 46, 48, 56]
- **Fit:** gap. It is FD-07 evidence, like LEGO (LEG-03).

### SBX-07: Mandated warmth

- **What the company does:** handwritten notes on every cup became an expectation in 2025, with discipline up to termination for not complying. The union says baristas write them "at metaphorical gunpoint"; Starbucks calls them "a meaningful way for our baristas to connect" [41, 42, 43].
- **Fit:** gap. A culture imposed through discipline rather than built with employees.
- **Candidate for rejection:** customer experience goals enforced by discipline, without employee input.

### SBX-08: Equity and tuition for part-timers

- **What the company does:**
  - **Bean Stock since 1991:** stock for all partners including part-timers; Starbucks says $2.75B in pre-tax gains have gone to more than 2M partners.
  - **College Achievement Plan:** full tuition, with more than 14,000 graduates.

  [3, 67, 68, 69]
- **Fit:** gap. Strong FD-02 evidence, comparable to Patagonia's early benefits (PAT-08).

### SBX-09: No union contract

- **What the company does:** the first union vote was in December 2021. Bargaining broke down in December 2024, and an open-ended strike began in November 2025. As of November 2026 there is still no first contract, and a consumer boycott is running [79, 80, 85, 87].
- **Fit:** differs. It is the most prolonged version of the pattern seen at Everlane (EVL-07) and Patagonia (PAT-09).
- **Candidate for rejection:** treating an employee organisation as an adversary.

### SBX-10: CEO pay ratio

- **What the company does:** FY2024 was 6,666:1 by the AFL-CIO calculation, mainly from replacing Niccol's Chipotle equity. FY2025 was 1,794:1, against a median employee (a part-time barista) on $17,279 [75, 77].
- **Fit:** gap. Compare Costco (COS-10) and Buffer's pay formula (BUF-03).

### SBX-11: Ethical sourcing with verification

- **What the company does:** C.A.F.E. Practices since 2004, with more than 200 indicators verified by a third party. 99.15 % of coffee met the standard in FY2025, published including the decline. "100 % ethically sourced" claims are challenged in court [91, 92, 93, 94].
- **Fit:** partial. A published, verified standard is what FD-08 recommends. The marketing claim goes beyond the data.

### SBX-12: Board oversight of labour, created and removed

- **What the company does:** a board committee was created in 2023 after a majority shareholder vote, and dissolved in November 2025. Shareholders filed in 2026 to restore it and to split the chair and CEO roles [82].
- **Fit:** partial. Governance responded to owners, then reversed.

### SBX-13: Activists drive a CEO change

- **What the company does:** Elliott and Starboard built stakes in 2024, and Narasimhan was replaced in August 2024 [13, 14, 15].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01 evidence alongside Southwest. In this case the new CEO moved back towards the core rather than away from it.

### SBX-14: Growth that eroded the product, twice

- **What the company does:** 2007: growth to 13,000 stores led to "commoditization". 2024: an efficiency focus led to falling traffic [4, 20, 56].
- **Fit:** gap. The strongest FD-07 evidence in this research.

### SBX-15: Loyalty rework

- **What the company does:** the Starbucks Rewards relaunch (March 2026) added tiers. Analysts say many Green and Gold members earn fewer Stars [51, 52].
- **Fit:** partial. A change to the value customers receive, presented as an improvement, without a published calculation of who gains and who loses.

## 4. Where the framework goes further than this company

- **Cost disclosure, public roadmaps, open project management:** none at Starbucks.
- **Community input as a standing process:** Starbucks had one and closed it.

## 5. Candidates for explicit rejection

- **SBX-09:** treating a union as an adversary. Reject.
- **SBX-07:** culture enforced by discipline. Reject.
- **SBX-05:** prices without costs. Reject, as with PAT-07.
- **SBX-14:** growth by store count and throughput without a quality limit. Reject.

## 6. Open questions for the decisions

- **FD-05:** should the framework require that community input channels can't be closed without notice, reasons and a replacement?
- **FD-07:** what measure would have warned Starbucks earlier? Traffic (transactions) rather than ticket?
- **FD-02:** is equity for all staff (SBX-08) enough employee voice, or is a recognised process needed (SBX-09)?
