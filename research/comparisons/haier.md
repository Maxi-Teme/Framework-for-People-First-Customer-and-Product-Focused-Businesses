## 1. Summary

Haier **confirms** community-driven product development through co-creation platforms. Users take part in design on COSMOPlat and HOPE, pre-orders test demand (150,000 for one washer), and GE Appliances' FirstBuild lab has turned 37 community products into mainstream ones.

It **adds** a structural answer to FD-05 that no other company in the archive offers:
- decisions pushed to thousands of small teams close to users;
- pay tied to user results;
- employees co-investing in their units;
- leaders who can be voted out;
- unit performance visible inside the company.

It **differs** from the framework in several places:
- the unit metrics stay internal, so nothing is published;
- there is no price transparency;
- a takedown notice against an open-source integration of its smart-home app (later withdrawn);
- a documented pressure culture;
- in 2026, re-centralisation when results fell.

Most of the evidence is self-described. Treat the model as a source of mechanisms to test, not as proven.

## 3. Practices in detail

### HAI-01: Microenterprises

- **What the company does:** by 2018 there were about 4,000 small units, each holding three rights: strategy, people and pay distribution. User-facing units can buy from outside instead of from internal units [10, 14].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-05: decisions placed with small teams close to users, instead of one accountable owner (Toyota's chief engineer, TOY-05).
- **Transfer:** for a small company, this mainly means giving the team that faces a customer segment real authority over its product and budget.

### HAI-02: Pay tied to user value

- **What the company does:** targets are set "outside in", as a multiple of market growth. Pay rises in tiers, and above a threshold the unit shares 20–40 % of the extra profit [14].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-02, comparable to Buffer's profit sharing (BUF-07) but per team and conditional.
- **Risk:** see HAI-10.

### HAI-03: Co-investment and removable leaders

- **What the company does:** teams invest their own money in new units. Missing baseline targets for three months replaces the leader automatically, and two-thirds of members can vote one out [14, 34].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01 and FD-02: employees as owners of their unit, with a vote over leadership.

### HAI-04: Internal performance transparency

- **What the company does:** "performance data for all MEs is transparent across the company" (as described) [14].
- **Fit:** partial. Full internal transparency, nothing public.

### HAI-05: User co-creation platforms

- **What the company does:**
  - **HOPE:** about 500 ideas a month; the Air Cube purifier was developed with 128 experts and "9.8 million users consulted".
  - **COSMOPlat:** users take part in design and production.
  - **Yunxi washer:** users took part from idea to launch, with 150,000 pre-orders.

  [34, 37, 39]
- **Fit:** match with principle 2, with pre-orders as the decision test, close to LEGO Ideas' threshold (LEG-01).

### HAI-06: FirstBuild

- **What the company does:** GE Appliances' co-creation lab has made 104 products and taken 37 into the main range, with about 245,000 "co-creators". The Opal ice maker raised more than $2.7M on Indiegogo first [42, 43].
- **Fit:** match. Crowdfunding as validation before production.
- **Transfer:** a small company can test a product with paid pre-orders before building it.

### HAI-07: Win-Win Value-Added statement

- **What the company does:** an internal report at unit level, mixing financial and user measures [36].
- **Fit:** partial. FD-06: a measure built around user value, but not public.

### HAI-08: Middle management removed

- **What the company does:** more than 10,000 middle managers removed, by various counts. A peer-reviewed study found more than 20,000 employees left within ten years because they "did not or could not buy into" the system [2, 10, 11, 13].
- **Fit:** gap. The cost of the transition falls on employees.

### HAI-09: Self-described evidence

- **What the company does:** most detail comes from Haier, its institute, or partners and advocates. A peer-reviewed study called judging the model's effect "somewhat premature" [11, 53, 54].
- **Fit:** partial. FD-06: claims about a model need outside verification.

### HAI-10: Pressure culture

- **What the company does:** TIME described "nonstop competition". In 2019 four employees were dismissed for sleeping after lunch, in a notice written in the model's language [2, 51].
- **Fit:** differs. FD-02: pay-for-results without protections.
- **Candidate for rejection:** team-level pay and removal mechanisms without limits that protect workers.

### HAI-11: Collective ownership

- **What the company does:** Haier Group "has no shareholders", and its board is chosen by a Staff Assembly. It controls about 34 % of the listed company. Related-party flows are large (Group finance company deposits capped at RMB 34B a day) [8, 15, 20, 58].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01: a non-investor ownership form, but opaque to outsiders.

### HAI-12: Re-centralisation

- **What the company does:** in 2026, the listed company merged business lines into large platforms for "unified dispatch of all resources", while results fell [48].
- **Fit:** gap. Evidence that decentralised models get pulled back under pressure.

### HAI-13: Takedown notice against open source

- **What the company does:** in January 2024 Haier Europe sent a cease-and-desist to the developer of an open-source Home Assistant integration. After a boycott call it withdrew and "sought dialogue" [65].
- **Fit:** differs from open, community-driven development.

### HAI-14: No pricing transparency

- **What the company does:** no pricing philosophy found; margins blamed on "price competition" [8, 29].
- **Fit:** differs from principle 3.

## 4. Where the framework goes further than this company

- Public metrics, costs and roadmaps: Haier keeps unit data internal.
- Open software and interfaces for users: Haier resisted them in 2024.

## 5. Candidates for explicit rejection

- **HAI-10:** pay and removal mechanisms without worker protections. Reject.
- **HAI-13:** suppressing community integrations. Reject.
- **HAI-14:** prices without costs. Reject.

## 6. Open questions for the decisions

- **FD-05:** one accountable owner (Toyota), a community threshold (LEGO), or small autonomous teams (Haier)? Can a small company combine one owner with team-level authority?
- **FD-02:** if pay is tied to results, what floor and protections does the framework require?
- **FD-06:** should claims about a company's model be verified by outsiders before they're used as evidence?
