## 1. Summary

Ben & Jerry's **adds** early, concrete mechanisms the framework doesn't have yet:
- community share ownership (a Vermont-only public offering in 1984);
- a fixed share of profits for the community (7.5 % of pre-tax profits);
- a pay-ratio cap (5:1, later 7:1);
- a living-wage policy;
- social audits published since the late 1980s, including an auditor's disclaimer.

Its main lesson is **cautionary**, for FD-01. The 2000 sale contract created an independent board to protect the mission. A quarter-century later the parent:
- changed the eligibility rules and removed most of that board;
- replaced the CEO;
- stopped funding the Foundation;
- obtained a court ruling that the board could sue only over its own composition.

A contract with the owner protects principles only while the owner chooses to honour it.

## 3. Practices in detail

### BNJ-01: Community share offering

- **What the company does:** in 1984 it offered 73,500 shares at $10.50, to Vermont residents only, with a minimum of 12 shares. About 1,800 households took part (Cohen: about 1 in 100 Vermont households) [30, 31, 32].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01: ownership by customers and neighbours. Compare Framework Computer's accredited-only community round (FRM-14).
- **Transfer:** a small company can raise from its community through a direct public offering or crowdfunding equity.

### BNJ-02: Fixed profit share for the community

- **What the company does:** 7.5 % of pre-tax profits to the Foundation from 1985; grants guided by employee committees [9, 21].
- **Fit:** gap. Compare Patagonia's 1 % of sales (PAT-03) and Buffer's profit sharing (BUF-07).

### BNJ-03: Pay-ratio cap

- **What the company does:** top pay capped at 5:1, later 7:1, of the lowest. It reached 17:1 by 2000 and was effectively abandoned to hire an outside CEO in the mid-1990s; CEO pay has been undisclosed since 2000 [27, 28].
- **Fit:** partial. FD-02: a published rule that worked while the company was small and founder-led.

### BNJ-04: Three-part mission

- **What the company does:** product, economic and social missions given equal weight since 1988 [21].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-07: an explicit statement that profit isn't the only goal.

### BNJ-05: Livable wage

- **What the company does:** a livable-wage methodology since 1995: $17.09 an hour in 2016, against a Vermont minimum of $9.60 [22].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-02, like Costco's pay strategy (COS-09).

### BNJ-06: Social audits

- **What the company does:** annual social and environmental reports since the late 1980s. In 2011 it published the auditor's disclaimer of opinion on its own social indicators [21, 38].
- **Fit:** match. Principle 1 applied to impact, including bad news about the measures themselves.

### BNJ-07: Values-led sourcing

- **What the company does:**
  - Caring Dairy.
  - Milk with Dignity: farmworker-designed standards, with more than $4.4M paid for farm improvements.
  - Fairtrade ingredients.
  - Brownies from Greyston's open-hiring bakery since 1987.

  [23, 33]
- **Fit:** gap. FD-08 and FD-02.

### BNJ-08: Mission protected by contract

- **What the company does:** section 6.14 of the 2000 merger agreement created an independent board responsible for "preserving and enhancing the objectives of the historical social mission". Its members could be removed only by the board itself [5, 7].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01: one of the options considered (investor agreements that lock in principles).

### BNJ-09: Contract unenforceable in practice

- **What the company does:** in August 2026 Judge Castel ruled that the independent directors may sue only under the board-composition clause, not over censorship or the CEO's removal. The Foundation "may not sue" over its allocation [2, 3].
- **Fit:** gap. The strongest evidence that a contract with the owner is weak protection.
- **Lesson for FD-01:** protections need a party with standing and its own resources, such as a foundation that owns shares or a golden share, not only rights under the buyer's contract.

### BNJ-10: Parent remakes the board

- **What the company does:**
  - **December 2025:** new term limits and a rule requiring certification of the parent's code of conduct made three directors ineligible.
  - **January 2026:** the rest were treated as having left after they refused to certify.
  - **CEO:** replaced in 2025.
  - **Speech:** the board alleges statements on Gaza, refugees and a post naming Trump were blocked.

  [6, 9, 16, 17]
- **Fit:** differs.
- **Candidate for rejection:** principles whose guardians can be redefined by the party they guard against.

### BNJ-11: Foundation defunded

- **What the company does:** funding was withheld after an audit in 2025. The Foundation was evicted and plans to suspend operations on 31 December 2026; TMICC says it remains committed to funding a foundation [18, 19].
- **Fit:** differs. A purpose share that depends on the owner's goodwill.

### BNJ-12: Customer input in flavours

- **What the company does:** flavour development is mostly internal, with "Flavor Gurus" taking 12 months or more. Customers contribute through idea submissions, a Fairtrade flavour contest (2010) and votes to resurrect flavours. A founder-crowdsourced flavour was rejected by management [30, 35, 36, 37].
- **Fit:** partial.

### BNJ-13: No pricing transparency

- **What the company does:** none found; investors asked for separate Ben & Jerry's figures [26].
- **Fit:** differs from principle 3.

### BNJ-14: Employee-ownership carve-out explored

- **What the company does:** in October 2026 the founders explored an employee-stock-ownership deal with a KKR executive. TMICC: "not for sale" [55].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01: employee ownership as a recovery route.

## 4. Where the framework goes further than this company

- Cost and price transparency; public roadmaps; open project management: none.
- Transparency of governance: CEO pay and the parent's decisions aren't disclosed.

## 5. Candidates for explicit rejection

- **BNJ-08 as the only protection:** mission protection by a contract with the buyer. Reject as sufficient; use ownership, a golden share or a funded foundation.
- **BNJ-10 / BNJ-11:** reject as outcomes to design against.
- **BNJ-13:** prices without costs. Reject.

## 6. Open questions for the decisions

- **FD-01:** which structures survive a sale: a golden share held by a foundation (the Ecosia model), steward ownership (Patagonia), employee ownership? Ben & Jerry's shows that a contract alone doesn't.
- **FD-02:** can a pay-ratio cap survive growth and outside hiring? It didn't here.
- **FD-01 / FD-08:** should a community profit share be held by a body with its own funds and legal standing?
