## 1. Summary

Nike **confirms** two of the framework's ideas in its history:
- **Product development with users.** Bowerman built and tested shoes with his runners.
- **Public supply-chain transparency under pressure.** In 2005 Nike was the first in its industry to publish every contract factory. Its list is now a quarterly map, and it publishes sustainability targets with results, including misses.

It also shows the limits. Transparency stopped at factory names, and didn't reach wages or audit results; in 2026 those are disputed again. Its digital "community" tools served scarcity and hype rather than co-creation: SNKRS drops and bots, and the .SWOOSH Web3 platform, launched as "co-create" and then shut down. It has never disclosed costs, and its tariff pass-through is challenged in court. Dual-class control kept activists out, but didn't stop strategy drift under professional CEOs.

## 3. Practices in detail

### NKE-01: Athlete-driven development

- **What the company does:** from 1958 Bowerman hand-made shoes for Oregon runners. He traced their feet and made new prototypes weekly; a runner's injury led to the redesign that became the Cortez, and runners logged more than 1,000 miles on prototypes. Nike: Bowerman shaped "Nike's ethos of leveraging athlete insights" [1].
- **Fit:** match with principle 2, in its simplest form: build with the people who use it.

### NKE-02: Public factory list

- **What the company does:** on 14 April 2005 Nike became "the first in its industry to voluntarily disclose the names and locations of the more than 700 active contract factories". It is now a quarterly Manufacturing Map; errors found by the press in 2022 were corrected [13, 22, 24].
- **Fit:** match. The strongest FD-08 evidence for supplier disclosure, nine years before Patagonia's comparable data.

### NKE-03: Crisis-driven commitments

- **What the company does:** Knight, 1998: "The Nike product has become synonymous with slave wages, forced overtime and arbitrary abuse." He made commitments on air quality, minimum age, NGO monitoring with public results, education and micro-loans. Wages were left out [17, 19].
- **Fit:** partial. Transparency arrived because of outside exposure, like Everlane's after 2020 (EVL-12).

### NKE-04: Private regulation

- **What the company does:** a code of conduct, "foundational expectations" (90 % of facilities met them in FY25 against 100 %), and FLA accreditation. The Oregonian (2026) reports "sham" audits and unrealistic injury figures [21, 25, 41].
- **Fit:** partial. Self-reported compliance without published audit results.

### NKE-05: Remedy disputes

- **What the company does:** wage theft claims at Violet Apparel (Cambodia) and Hong Seng (Thailand). Nike rejects the first; for the second, it agreed in 2025 that remedy was due [35, 37, 38].
- **Fit:** differs. FD-02 and FD-08: responsibility for supplier workers' pay.

### NKE-06: Distribution swings

- **What the company does:** the Consumer Direct Offense (2017) focused on about 40 of about 30,000 retail partners. Donahoe (2024): Nike had "over-rotated away from wholesale" [49, 56].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-07 evidence: a growth strategy that damaged the business.

### NKE-07: Engineered scarcity

- **What the company does:** SNKRS app drops. In 2021 an executive resigned after it emerged that her son ran a bot-driven resale business. Bloomberg said SNKRS "gamified Nike's engineered-scarcity model" [66, 67].
- **Fit:** differs from fair pricing: scarcity used to drive demand and resale value.

### NKE-08: .SWOOSH

- **What the company does:** launched in 2022 with the promise that users could "co-create" virtual products with Nike designers. RTFKT was wound down in December 2024, and a proposed class action alleges a "rug pull" [68, 69, 71].
- **Fit:** differs. Community co-creation as a marketing promise that ended at the company's discretion. The same lesson as My Starbucks Idea (SBX-01), with money involved.

### NKE-09: Personalisation

- **What the company does:** NIKEiD (1999), now Nike By You, lets customers configure products [65].
- **Fit:** partial. Individual choice, not community input into the product line.

### NKE-10: Pricing

- **What the company does:**
  - No cost disclosure, ever.
  - "Pricing power" cited by the CFO in 2024.
  - Tariff price rises from 2025, then $986M of refunds booked in 2026. A proposed class action alleges "double recovery".

  [87, 91, 92]
- **Fit:** differs. Compare Costco's "partial reinvestment" (COS-06) and Framework Computer's automatic pass-through (FRM-03).

### NKE-11: Dual-class control

- **What the company does:** Class A elects three-quarters of the board. Swoosh, LLC holds about 79 % of Class A, and Travis Knight holds 2 of its 5 votes [93, 95, 96, 100]. At the 2026 meeting the 8 Class A directors were elected with no votes withheld [103].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01: it shields the company from activists (compare Southwest, SWA-06), but concentrates control without making it accountable to customers or workers.

### NKE-12: Workplace culture exposed from inside

- **What the company does:** in 2018 a survey by senior women ("Starfish") led to executive departures and pay adjustments for about 10 % of staff. Unsealed records show a 2018 pay-equity promotion analysis was halted on counsel's advice without telling staff [73, 74, 75, 79].
- **Fit:** partial. Employees forced the issue; the company didn't publish what it found.

### NKE-13: Buybacks during the decline

- **What the company does:** $12.1B of an $18B programme spent by May 2026, paused from Q1 FY26 [27].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01 and FD-07, like Boeing (BOE-03).

### NKE-14: Sustainability targets and results

- **What the company does:** it published 2025 targets and results, including misses (51 % preferred materials; owned-facility emissions −77 %) [29].
- **Fit:** partial. A public target with a public result, like Patagonia (PAT-06).

## 4. Where the framework goes further than this company

- **Cost and price transparency:** none.
- **Public roadmap and open project management:** none.
- **Supply-chain transparency beyond names:** wages and audit results are not published.

## 5. Candidates for explicit rejection

- **NKE-07:** engineered scarcity. Reject.
- **NKE-08:** community co-creation that can be withdrawn at will. Reject in this form.
- **NKE-10:** prices without costs. Reject, as with PAT-07.

## 6. Open questions for the decisions

- **FD-08:** is publishing supplier names enough, or must wages and audit results follow?
- **FD-05:** should the framework require that co-creation platforms have exit terms that protect participants?
