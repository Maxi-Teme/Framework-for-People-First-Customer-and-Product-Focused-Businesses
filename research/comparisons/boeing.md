## 1. Summary

Boeing **confirms** customer co-design as a product process. Eight airlines helped design the 777 through a structured questionnaire, an agreed baseline and a written objectives agreement. That makes it the oldest working example of principle 2 in this research.

Its 737 MAX years are the **strongest negative evidence** for principle 1. Per the House committee, Boeing hid a flight-control system from pilots, withheld test data from its regulator, and ignored pilot input; 346 people died. The cause was a deliberate shift from engineering to financial goals: "run like a business rather than a great engineering firm". Buybacks, outsourcing and supplier squeezes followed.

The rebuild under Ortberg **adds** mechanisms the framework lacks:
- six named safety and quality KPIs, reviewed by the regulator;
- a reporting channel for safety concerns;
- values drafted by employees;
- contracts that cap mandatory overtime and set up a joint committee on AI.

But Boeing keeps the KPI values and absolute report numbers private. Lesson for FD-06: a measure is only accountable if its value is published.

## 3. Practices in detail

### BOE-01: 777 "Working Together"

- **What the company does:** in January 1990, eight airlines received a 23-page questionnaire on what they wanted. By March they had agreed a baseline configuration with Boeing. United's one-page objectives agreement, signed by Boeing executives, gave it "much greater input into planning and design" in return for an order [3, 4].
- **Why it worked:** customers with deep technical knowledge, a single product, and executives who signed up to the process.
- **Fit:** match with principle 2. It is a structured path from customer input to design decisions.
- **Transfer:** a short written agreement on goals with the first customers, plus a structured questionnaire, suits a small company too.

### BOE-02: "Run like a business"

- **What the company does:** Stonecipher (2004): "When people say I changed the culture of Boeing, that was the intent, so that it's run like a business rather than a great engineering firm. It is a great engineering firm, but people invest in a company because they want to make money." [6]
- **Fit:** gap. It is the opposite of a long-term, product-first principle (FD-07). It parallels Starbucks' "manufacturing facility" (SBX-14).
- **Candidate for rejection:** financial returns as the governing principle above the product.

### BOE-03: Buybacks over engineering

- **What the company does:** $43.5B of buybacks in 2013–2019, about all of profit by one count, while R&D and workforce shrank as a share of sales [10, 11, 13].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01 and FD-07 evidence: where profits go is a principle question.

### BOE-04: Concealment

- **What the company does:** per the House committee:
  - MCAS was hidden from pilots and not classified as safety-critical;
  - Boeing didn't disclose that a safety alert was inoperable on most of the fleet;
  - it withheld simulator data showing a pilot took more than 10 seconds to react;
  - FAA-delegated staff at Boeing didn't alert the FAA.

  [15]
- **Fit:** differs from principle 1, with the worst consequences in this research.
- **Candidate for rejection:** the framework's minimum public set (FD-03) must include safety-relevant information for users and regulators, without exception.

### BOE-05: Messages released under pressure

- **What the company does:** in 2020 Boeing released hundreds of internal messages to Congress and the FAA. Among them: "designed by clowns who in turn are supervised by monkeys". Some had been shared earlier with investigators but not the FAA [48, 49].
- **Fit:** partial. Disclosure came late and under compulsion.

### BOE-06: Named KPIs, unpublished values

- **What the company does:** the Safety & Quality Plan (May 2024) names six KPIs. They include employee proficiency, rework hours, supplier shortages, unfinished jobs at rollout and defects before delivery. The FAA reviews them weekly and monthly. Boeing publishes selective claims ("rework… by 60 %") but not the values. A Senate hearing record says the data had been tracked "for years" [55, 56, 57, 58, 59].
- **Fit:** partial. It is the measures FD-06 asks for, with the public part missing.
- **Transfer:** name the measures *and* publish their values on a fixed schedule.

### BOE-07: Speak Up

- **What the company does:** reports rose more than 500 % in early 2024 and 220 % from 2023 to 2024, published as percentages only. The engineers' union asked "220 % of what to what?" and still alleged retaliation in 2026 [60, 61, 62].
- **Fit:** partial. It is the internal half of FD-03's "problems become visible at once", without absolute numbers or outcomes.

### BOE-08: Values written by employees

- **What the company does:** after an employee survey with an 82 % response rate, which was "brutal to leadership", a working group of 40 employees drafted five values (2025) [63, 64].
- **Fit:** gap. A process for setting values with staff, the reverse of GitLab's top-down retirement (GIT-03).

### BOE-09: Suppliers squeezed, then supported

- **What the company does:** "Partnering for Success" asked for 15 % price cuts and slowed payments. Under Ortberg, suppliers were supported through the strike and Spirit was bought back. One supplier: "The Boeing mantra of 'You do it my way or you're done'… is almost gone." [41, 69, 77]
- **Fit:** gap. FD-02 evidence on supplier terms.

### BOE-10: Risk pushed onto suppliers

- **What the company does:** the 787 supply chain was designed to cut development time and cost by passing risk to suppliers. It ran three years late and billions over budget (secondary) [9].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-07: outsourcing as a short-term cost play, like LEGO's reversed outsourcing (LEG-14).

### BOE-11: Engineers' contract

- **What the company does:** engineers rejected an offer with the largest wage pool since 1983 out of mistrust. A second offer was ratified in October 2026. It includes:
  - a 10 % raise;
  - mandatory overtime capped (144 → 96 hours a quarter for engineers);
  - three extra days of leave;
  - a joint labour-management committee on AI.

  Negotiators want engineers back "to the center of Boeing's universe" [44, 45].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-02 evidence: a contract that limits workload and gives staff a say in how AI is introduced.

### BOE-12: Bonus score

- **What the company does:** one bonus score for more than 100,000 employees, 80 % financial and 20 % safety and execution. The 2025 payout was 131 % of target [58, 65].
- **Fit:** partial. Safety in the bonus, but weighted far below finance.

### BOE-13: Pilot input ignored

- **What the company does:** the FAA expert panel found Boeing "does not appropriately take pilot input". Southwest's pilots sued, saying they were "deliberately misled" [66, 67].
- **Fit:** differs from principle 2. The same company that co-designed with airlines in 1990 shut out pilots.

### BOE-14: Capital to the balance sheet

- **What the company does:** buybacks and dividends have been suspended since March 2020. A $24.25B equity raise followed in 2024; capital now goes to debt reduction and capacity [12, 17, 36].
- **Fit:** gap. FD-01: a choice of capital use that can reverse once finances recover.

### BOE-15: Accountability by agreement

- **What the company does:** the 2025 non-prosecution agreement includes fines, a victims' fund and more than $455M for compliance. It uses a consultant chosen by Boeing instead of an independent monitor. The judge said it "fails to secure the necessary accountability" [18, 26, 27].
- **Fit:** partial. FD-01 and FD-03: who checks the company matters.

## 4. Where the framework goes further than this company

- **Public measures:** Boeing names KPIs but keeps the values private.
- **Public roadmaps and open project management:** none.
- **Pricing transparency:** none (aircraft prices are negotiated and confidential).

## 5. Candidates for explicit rejection

- **BOE-04:** concealment of safety-relevant information. Reject.
- **BOE-02 / BOE-03:** financial returns as the governing principle; buybacks over engineering. Reject.
- **BOE-13:** shutting out expert users. Reject.

## 6. Open questions for the decisions

- **FD-06:** publish measure values on a fixed schedule, not just measure names?
- **FD-05:** a written objectives agreement with first customers (BOE-01) as a lightweight mechanism?
- **FD-02:** should employees have a formal say in how automation and AI are introduced (BOE-11)?
