# Framework for Open and Community-Driven Business Operations and Product Development

> **Status: work in progress (revised 2026-11-23).** This framework is being reworked on the basis of comparative studies of 15 companies, including Toyota, Buffer, GitLab, Framework Computer, LEGO, Costco, Starbucks, Boeing, Southwest Airlines, Everlane, Nike, Patagonia, Haier, Ben & Jerry's and Fairphone. Every principle is treated as a hypothesis and changes when the evidence says it doesn't serve the goal. Decisions so far are listed under [Revision log](#revision-log). The name will be revisited once the principles are settled.

## Goal

A business with a **real customer and product focus that keeps it over time**.

The studies show that customer and product focus holds where it is built into the business model and the ownership, and that it is usually lost through growth and efficiency drives, rarely through a lack of openness. Openness and community involvement are therefore **means**, not the goal. Their purpose is to make the company's commitments checkable and to keep it close to the people who use its products.

## Core Principles

1. **Transparency and accountability: every commitment is public and checkable.** *(decided 2026-11-23)*

   - **Always public (first-order):**
     - safety-relevant information for users and the public;
     - the company's commitments (its mission and its promises) and their results, including misses;
     - the outcomes of audits and checks on those commitments;
     - ownership and governance: who owns and controls the company, and the rules that protect its mission.
   - **Public, or proven fair by an independent route (second-order):** cost and price breakdowns, supplier lists, salaries and detailed financials.
     - They need not be published if the fairness they would show is proven in another way.
     - Examples of such routes: a published price rule, certification or a third-party audit of suppliers and emissions, independent employee reviews, or a union agreement.
   - Commitments are changed only in public, with the reason. Values that can be dropped silently don't count.
   - Transparency is aimed: it exists to make commitments checkable by the people they affect, not as an end in itself. Publishing everything can harm people (for example individual salaries by name).

2. **Community-driven product development.** *(under review)*

   - Product development is open to public scrutiny and input, ensuring that products are tailored to meet customer needs effectively.
   - Customers' insights and feedback drive continuous improvement, resulting in products that are high-quality and well-suited to market demands.
   - Community involvement fosters innovation and provides diverse perspectives, enhancing the product's relevance and appeal.
   - *Under review:* whether community input is required, or one of several ways to stay close to users. Other routes include direct observation of users, co-design with lead customers, and pre-orders. In each case the path from input to decision and the reasons for decisions would be published.

3. **Fair pricing: publish the price rule and prove it is kept.** *(decided 2026-11-23)*

   - The company publishes a price rule set in advance. Examples: a margin cap; cost changes passed on in both directions, including price cuts and refunds when costs fall; profit taken from a refundable fee rather than from the goods.
   - It proves the rule is kept, by either route:
     - its own published figures, enough to check the rule (for example average margin or the cost changes behind a price change); or
     - an independent audit whose result is public.
   - A full cost breakdown is welcome but not required. Under principle 1, costs are second-order information.
   - A price rule is a commitment: it changes only in public, with the reason and the analysis behind it.

4. **Open project management.** *(under review)*
   - Project management elements, including task boards and roadmaps, should be open to the public and open for discussion.
   - Open project management allows stakeholders to follow the project's progress, contribute ideas, and provide feedback on priorities and timelines.
   - *Under review:* whether full task boards and roadmaps are needed, or a public list of the problems being worked on and the reasons for decisions.

### Candidate principles (in development)

The studies point to three areas the original draft did not cover. Each is being developed into a principle:

- **Ownership and capital.** How the principles are protected when owners or investors want something else. Principles held only by a founder, a company culture or a contract with the buyer were reversed when ownership or control changed. Principles anchored in ownership, statutes or binding public commitments held.
- **Employees and suppliers.** How people are paid, developed and heard, and how suppliers are treated. Fair pay and a real voice for employees, and fair, long-term supplier terms.
- **Long-term horizon and growth.** Decisions take the long view even at short-term cost, and growth is limited by the company's ability to keep quality and develop its people.

## Implementation Strategies

1. **Communication and engagement:**

   - Establish clear communication channels to keep stakeholders informed about project updates, decisions, and opportunities for participation.
   - Encourage active engagement through forums, feedback mechanisms, and regular updates.

2. **Documentation and accessibility:**

   - Document commitments, decisions and their reasons in formats the public can understand.
   - Keep the documentation current, and record changes with their reasons.

3. **Public commitments with measures:**

   - For each commitment, publish how it is measured and report the results on a fixed schedule, including misses.
   - Where something stays private under principle 1, publish which independent route proves its fairness.

4. **Continuous improvement:**
   - Embrace a culture of continuous improvement based on feedback and data-driven insights from customers and the community.
   - Regularly evaluate and adapt governance structures, product features and pricing to meet evolving customer needs, without letting growth erode quality.

## Benefits

1. **Trust that can be checked:** customers and other stakeholders can verify that commitments are kept instead of having to take them on faith.
2. **Products that stay close to users:** a defined path from user input to decisions keeps the product relevant.
3. **Resilience:** principles anchored in ownership and public commitments are harder to drop under pressure from growth, investors or a change of leadership.

## Conclusion

This framework describes how to build and keep a company with a real customer and product focus. Openness, community involvement and published price rules are tools for that goal. They are kept where the evidence shows they work, and changed where it shows they don't.

## Footer: Understanding Stakeholders

In the context of this framework document, **stakeholders** refer to individuals or groups who have an interest or concern in the business or project being discussed. They encompass:

1. **Customers:** Those who use or intend to use the products or services offered by the business.
2. **Community Members:** Individuals or organizations within the broader community impacted by the business's activities, decisions, or products.
3. **Investors:** Individuals or entities who have invested financially in the business and therefore have a stake in its success and profitability.
4. **Employees:** People working within the business who contribute directly to its operations and success.
5. **Suppliers and Partners:** Entities that provide goods, services, or collaborate with the business to deliver products or services to customers.
6. **Regulators and Government Agencies:** Entities responsible for overseeing and regulating aspects of the business, ensuring compliance with laws and regulations.
7. **Competitors:** Other businesses within the same industry or market space whose actions and strategies may impact the business in question.
8. **Media and Public Opinion:** Journalists, influencers, and public opinion holders who can influence the reputation and perception of the business.

Understanding and effectively managing relationships with these stakeholders is crucial for maintaining trust and ensuring the long-term sustainability and success of the business or project.

Ownership, employees and suppliers, and long-term horizon and growth were added as candidate principles. The name will be revisited once the principles are settled.
- **2026-11-23 — Transparency is a means.** Principle 1 was rewritten. First-order information (safety, commitments and results, audit outcomes, ownership and governance) is always public. Second-order information (costs, suppliers, pay, detailed financials) is public or proven fair by an independent route. Rejected: hiding safety-relevant information, and values that can be retired without a public record.
- **2026-11-23 — Fair pricing through a rule.** Principle 3 was rewritten. Instead of disclosing all costs, the company publishes a price rule set in advance and proves it is kept, by its own figures or an independent audit. Reason: cost breakdowns faded as companies grew or didn't stop price rises; fairness held where a rule constrained prices. Customer guarantees (refunds, no charge for unused service) are still being worked out.
